# Ficha's words in each language

Every text the app shows is translated into Ficha's languages, which `scripts/languages.txt` lists, each from the change that first translates the catalogs into it (ADR 0027), and a term reads the same on every screen. This list holds the word each language uses for each term the UI shows: a new text uses the words here, and a new term gets a row in the change that brings it.

A term's source says where its words come from:

- **Anki**: Anki's own translation, from its Fluent files in [`ankitects/anki-core-i18n`](https://github.com/ankitects/anki-core-i18n), `core/<locale>/*.ftl`, under the message ID the row names. Where Anki has two words for one term, the row picks one.
- **Apple**: the word macOS uses in that language, in its own menus, System Settings and Finder. For Spanish, a Mac in Spanish (Latin America), `es_419` in macOS's own files, whose words are the ones Rioplatense Spanish uses ("Configuración", "Agregar", "respaldo"), not a Mac in Spain's Spanish ("Ajustes", "Añadir", "copia de seguridad"): Lucas's call, on 2 November 2026. For Portuguese, a Mac in Portuguese, which is Brazil's (`pt` in macOS's own files, where `pt_PT` is Portugal's).
- **Ficha**: our own word, chosen in the first translation into that language.

A language gets its column here in the change that translates the catalogs into it. How each addresses the user, and its quotes:

- Spanish is Rioplatense, with "vos" in what the app asks of the user ("Elegí", "Revisá", "Tenés"), and Anki's words stay as Anki's Spanish has them, but where a row says otherwise. Quotes are « ».
- German says "du", as Apple's German does, with quotes „ “.
- French says "vous", as Apple's French does, with quotes « » and a no-break space inside them and before : ; ? and !.
- Italian says "tu", as Apple's Italian does, with quotes “ ”.
- Portuguese is Brazil's, with "você" and quotes “ ”, and Anki's words from Anki's `pt-BR`.
- Russian says "Вы", capitalized, and writes е for ё, as Apple's Russian does, with quotes « ».
- Japanese is polite, with です and ます, as Apple's Japanese is, with quotes 「」.
- Korean is formal, with 하십시오체, as Apple's Korean is, with quotes ‘ ’. A term Anki's Korean leaves untranslated gets our word, which its row says.
- Simplified Chinese says 你, as Apple's does, with quotes “ ”, full-width punctuation and no space between Chinese and Latin letters or digits ("你的Mac", "%lld张卡片"), as Apple's `zh_CN` writes them, and Anki's words from Anki's `zh-CN`.
- Traditional Chinese, for Taiwan and Hong Kong alike, says 你 and takes Taiwan's words, as Apple's `zh_TW` does, with quotes 「」, ⋯ for every ellipsis ("設定⋯") and no space between Chinese and Latin letters or digits, and Anki's words from Anki's `zh-TW`.
- Arabic speaks to the user in the masculine singular, as Apple's Arabic does, with Apple's straight quotes "…", never « », and Anki's words from Anki's `ar`. Every text starts with an Arabic word, so it reads right to left whatever else it holds; a left-to-right mark keeps "⌘[" and the "#" of "#columns" in their order, and a no-break space joins الـ to the Latin word or number after it ("الـ Mac"), as Apple's Arabic writes it. The parts of a line that English joins with " · ", such as the menu's counts, are joined with "، ", as Apple's Arabic joins a list of counts and the parts of a date: "·" beside Arabic-Indic digits reads as ٠. So Diagnostics' "%@ · %@" is translated, "·" in every other language.
