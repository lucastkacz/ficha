# 03 Medicine

Sources read: 35 through WebFetch, plus about 15 searches whose extracts showed some 50 Student Doctor Network (SDN) threads second-hand. Covered from 2013 (SDN extracts; 2019 for what I read) to 2026.

SDN refused every fetch, so each SDN finding below is second-hand, from search extracts. Their ages come from the search tool: about 2019 to 2023 for the Anki threads, 2013 to 2018 for the "alternatives" threads. All are `[OLD]`.

## Findings

### How they use it: decks, volume, AnkiHub, what they pay

- **Adoption is high in the US, and lower elsewhere.**
  - `[SAID]` 68.3% (285 of 417) in a national survey of 560 students at 102 schools, 2020 data (Halperin 2024). Seen in [search extracts](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s40670-023-01826-8) and a [seller's blog, Sep 2026](https://studycardsai.com/blog/medical-student-anki-statistics) that sells AI cards.
  - `[SAID]` 94% of first-years at one school, of whom 97.6% used premade cards ([UCF, Oct 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12662189/), abstract extract only).
  - `[SAID]` About 80% in a [cohort of 53 in Ireland, 2025](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/an-assessment-of-anki-flashcards-use-in-comparison-to-alternative/).
  - `[SAID]` Only 25.2% (80 of 318) in [Jordan, 2026](https://www.synapsesocial.com/papers/69bf8692f665edcd009e8ef6).
- **AnKing is the default deck.**
  - `[SAID]` A forum reply calls it the most common and most used ([AnkiHub, Feb 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/looking-for-best-anki-decks-for-medical-students/408019)).
  - It holds about 35,096 cards ([AnkiHub, 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/study-methods-advice/574718)); `[CLAIMED]` "over 30k" ([AnkiHub](https://www.ankihub.net/)).
  - `[SAID]` Most users unsuspend cards by block or tag rather than study it all: SDN, about 6 threads ([example](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/is-anking-too-much-with-p-f-step-1.1444655/)) `[OLD]`.
- **Daily volume is in the hundreds.** Every figure below is self-reported.
  - `[SAID]` 400 a day is called normal, and one peer ran 600 with peaks near 1,000 ([AnkiHub, Jun-Oct 2026](https://community.ankihub.net/t/entering-ms2-in-fsrs-review-hell-400-reviews-day-need-optimization-advice/601366)).
  - `[SAID]` 300-600, rising to 800-1,000 near exams ([AnkiHub, 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/study-methods-advice/574718)).
  - `[SAID]` 900+ a day for about 2 hours, 500-700 in second year, about 1,200 for 60 days ([copy of r/medicalschoolanki, Mar 2025](https://sites.google.com/view/usmleprivateers/journal-of-reddit/medicalschool/2025/march/anki-14032025), second-hand).
  - `[SAID]` SDN ranges from under 300 up to 2,000 ([example](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/how-many-reviews-a-day-is-too-many.1440459/)) `[OLD]`.
  - `[SAID]` Anki users averaged 17.8 hours a week, SD 14.9 (Halperin, via the seller's blog above).
  - `[CLAIMED]` Strong scorers do 200-350, and 700+ far from the exam is a warning sign ([Residency Advisor, Jan 2026](https://residencyadvisor.com/resources/med-school-life/is-anki-enough-for-usmle-what-the-evidence-and-outcomes-show), which promotes its own tool; its survey figures are unverifiable).
  - In short, about 5 sources put a typical pre-clinical day at 300-700 reviews and 1.5-2+ hours.
- **Volume falls in the clinical years, and more in residency.**
  - `[SAID]` 120-180 a day in clinical years (SDN, [search set](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/how-many-reviews-a-day-is-too-many.1440459/)) `[OLD]`; about 200 in third year with FSRS (Reddit copy above, second-hand).
  - `[SAID]` One resident converted a board book and does 5 cards a day ([SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/is-anki-sustainable.1285926/)) `[OLD]`.
  - `[SAID]` Duke ob-gyn residents: 18 of 31 used an 883-card deck, median 276 cards over 3.7 hours ([Tsai 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40670-021-01320-z), extract) `[OLD]`.
  - `[CLAIMED]` Specialty decks run from 140 to 2,051 cards per topic ([EyeRounds, 2024](https://eyerounds.org/anki_cards/anki_home.html), free) to 5,185 ([NeurAnki, 2,241+ downloads, 2025](https://index.mirasmart.com/AAN2025/PDFfiles/AAN2025-002054.html), free).
- **They pay for a maintained deck, not for cards or scheduling.**
  - `[CLAIMED]` AnkiHub: Free $0, Core $6/mo, Premium with AI $10/mo, Lifetime $450 ([AnkiHub](https://www.ankihub.net/)). Its [FAQ](https://www.ankihub.net/faq) still says $5/mo, and offers scholarships.
  - `[SAID]` V12 of the Step deck needs a paid plan and V11 stays free, which surprised at least one user ([AnkiHub, Sep 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/can-t-download-anking-v12-step-deck-without-membership/555778)); others report paying $66 a year ([AnkiHub, Mar 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/subscription-and-syncing/433424)).
  - `[SAID]` One user calls about $6 good value, another notes the deck used to be free, and a $300 rival deck drew wide criticism (Reddit copy, second-hand).
  - `[SAID]` Early reaction to Premium's AI, first page only: 2 positive, 4 complaints, mostly about the button and window getting in the way ([AnkiHub, Nov 2024](https://community.ankihub.net/t/pre-release-powerful-new-features-with-ankihub-premium/343224)).
  - `[SAID]` AnkiMobile is a one-off $24.99 and AnkiDroid is free ([AnkiHub search set](https://community.ankihub.net/t/anki-on-iphone/469310)).
  - `[SAID]` For scale, one student listed $1,522 for study resources and spent $1,844 in all, including exams, by sharing: UWorld $419, Sketchy $400 shared by ten, AMBOSS $299 ([student blog, about 2022](https://frog-celery-52s6.squarespace.com/blog/cost-to-prep-for-step-1-comlex)) `[OLD]`.

### What they value, complain about, and why they quit

- **Value: consistency and the score link, though the evidence is mixed.**
  - `[SAID]` Daily users had median Step 1 scores of 238 against 233.5, with no gain on Step 2 ([Wothe 2023, n=165](https://doaj.org/article/7aaf357db5d54ad39fea3f672ad5aae4), extract).
  - `[SAID]` More cards a day and a higher share of self-made cards went with better results, while Anki use alone did not ([IAMSE abstract, Jun 2024](https://julnet.swoogo.com/iamse2024/session/2221298/an-analysis-of-anki-flashcard-application-usage-in-the-preclerkship-medical-school-curriculum-and-usmle-step-1-preparation)).
  - `[SAID]` 82.5% of Jordanian users felt it improved their performance (Jordan paper above).
  - `[SAID]` Residents liked fitting study between daily activities (Duke study, as a [review](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202307.1955) quotes it, extract).
- **Complaint 1, by far the loudest: review load and backlog.**
  - `[SAID]` About 10 SDN threads ([fatigue](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/anki-fatigue.1391194/), [1000s due](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/i-neglected-anking-over-break-and-have-1000s-of-due-cards-feeling-overwhelmed.1473656/)) `[OLD]`.
  - `[SAID]` 3 of the 7 AnkiHub threads I read; one backlog reached 7,500 after three months away ([Feb 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/post-step-1-review-overload/405496)).
  - `[SAID]` 82.1% of UCF users found it overwhelming to some extent, and 34.5% said it cost them sleep. I got this only through the seller's blog above and could not check it against the paper.
  - `[SAID]` "Potential for burnout" also appears in the Jordan paper.
- **Complaint 2: knowing the card rather than the medicine.**
  - `[SAID]` About 4 SDN threads ([overhyped](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/anyone-else-feel-like-anki-is-overhyped.1199012/)) `[OLD]`.
  - `[SAID]` A student who quit Zanki, on a [test-prep seller's site, about 2022](https://www.yousmle.com/zanki-brosencephalon-review/) `[OLD]`.
- **Complaint 3: friction.**
  - `[SAID]` Interface: [SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/i-hate-anki-am-i-crazy.1349771/) `[OLD]`.
  - `[SAID]` AnkiHub's add-on needs a desktop for the first setup, and phones only sync after that ([Mar 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/subscription-and-syncing/433424)).
  - `[SAID]` Adding cards on a tablet is slow ([AnkiHub search set](https://community.ankihub.net/t/ankimobile-ankidroid-with-ankihub-workaround/2227)).
  - `[SAID]` Leeches, sibling cards that give answers away, and about 30 seconds a card (Reddit copy, second-hand).
- **Why they quit, by rough frequency across the sources above:**
  - Time cost and backlog, about 12 mentions.
  - Step 1 going pass/fail, which makes the full deck feel like overkill, about 4 ([SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/is-anking-too-much-with-p-f-step-1.1444655/)) `[OLD]`.
  - Clerkships: `[SAID]` one student dropped it on internal medicine because of too many cards ([SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/how-to-study-for-third-year-anki-question-banks-or-both.1465775)) `[OLD]`; `[CLAIMED]` a [Sketchy blog, 2026](https://www.sketchy.com/blog/how-to-use-sketchy-in-the-clinical-years), sells a competing resource and says some stop on rotations.
  - Weak transfer to questions, about 4.
  - Stopping after Step 2, then regretting it (Reddit copy, second-hand).
- **Most advice is to shrink the load rather than quit.** `[SAID]` Fewer new cards, suspend, lower retention ([AnkiHub, 2026](https://community.ankihub.net/t/entering-ms2-in-fsrs-review-hell-400-reviews-day-need-optimization-advice/601366)). No reply told the busy student on the [Anki forum (Dec 2023)](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/is-it-pointless-for-me-to-use-anki-too-busy-in-med-school/38771) to quit.

### Alongside it or instead of it

- **Anki sits inside a stack, beside question banks.**
  - `[SAID]` UWorld, AMBOSS, Sketchy, Pathoma, Boards and Beyond, First Aid and NBME exams keep appearing with it ([AnkiHub, 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/study-methods-advice/574718); Reddit copy; [SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/thoughts-on-pathoma-sketchy-uworld-only-for-step-1.1487826/) `[OLD]`).
  - `[CLAIMED]` Anki should supplement questions ([Blueprint, Jan 2024](https://blog.blueprintprep.com/medical/5-faqs-about-using-anki-for-usmle-prep/), sells prep).
- **Competitors plug into Anki.**
  - `[CLAIMED]` AMBOSS claims 2 of 3 US students use it, and says many use its Anki add-on ([AMBOSS](https://ambossador.us.production.amboss.com/us/institutions/usage-report)).
  - `[CLAIMED]` Picmonic has an [add-on](https://www.picmonic.com/pages/picmonic-anki-win/), and its guest author moved to Picmonic's own daily quiz (2022) `[OLD]`.
  - `[CLAIMED]` First Aid Forward and Boards and Beyond link into AnkiHub ([AnkiHub](https://www.ankihub.net/)).
- **Replacements are rare and mostly old or sold.**
  - `[SAID]` Quizlet, Firecracker, Picmonic and Memorang appear in [SDN threads](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/firecracker-vs-memorang-vs-osmosis-vs-anki.1257339/) from about 2013-2018 `[OLD]`.
  - `[CLAIMED]` Recent "alternative" pages are ads: [iatroX, Mar 2026](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/anki-vs-quizlet-vs-spaced-repetition-apps-medical-students-2026) and [Neural Consult, May 2026](https://www.neuralconsult.com/blogs/neural-consult-vs-anki-for-medical-students).
  - `[SAID]` Students who leave usually move to question banks, not another flashcard app (about 4 SDN threads) `[OLD]`.
- **Lecturio:** it appeared in none of my results. I can say nothing about it.
- **A second, lighter tool: I found nobody asking for one.** That is 0 explicit requests in the 35 sources and about 50 SDN extracts. `[INFERRED]` People keep small things as a separate deck inside Anki ([AnkiHub](https://community.ankihub.net/t/wanting-to-create-subdecks-for-my-med-schoolblocks/296218), [Anki forum](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/how-to-study-just-one-deck-from-anking-separately-from-regular-cards/56088)). This is absence of evidence from sources that mostly can't be fully read, and Reddit was closed to me.

### Small moments, reminders, single facts

- **Phone gaps are real but thinly documented.**
  - `[SAID]` 2 SDN students: 10-20 cards on a bus or while waiting, and 5-minute stretches on busy days ([search set](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/the-official-anki-thread.1061415/)) `[OLD]`.
  - `[SAID]` Residents fit study between activities (Duke, above).
  - `[CLAIMED]` A [seller's blog](https://studycardsai.com/blog/anki-remote-for-med-school) recommends the phone for lines and short breaks, and a remote for long sessions.
  - `[INFERRED]` No study I found measures between-patient review on a phone. The search tool also showed a student describing downtime between cases, which I could not attribute to a source, so I don't count it.
- **Long sessions are sit-down work.** `[SAID]` Remotes, game controllers and a Mac turned to portrait appear in the Reddit copy (second-hand) and in [AnkiHub's own lesson](https://community.ankihub.net/t/lesson-15-using-shortcuts-on-ankimobile-droid-with-a-gamepad-controller/595884).
- **The ward may discourage visible phones.** `[SAID]` Third- and fourth-year students at one school held back from even professional phone use on rounds, fearing repercussions; 75% would use phones more with formal guidelines ([Jefferson, 2020](https://jdc.jefferson.edu/si_me_2022_phase1/21)) `[OLD]`. It says nothing about Anki itself, and rounds are not hallways.
- **Widgets and notifications: requested in general, not by medics.**
  - `[SAID]` 11 users in the [widget thread (2023-25)](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ankimobile-widget/32633), 12 for an [Apple Watch app (2021-23)](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/feature-request-apple-watch-version/10031) `[OLD]`, 3 for [notifications (2025)](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/functional-notifications-are-essential-for-ankimobile/66148), 2 in [another thread (2025)](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ios-widgets-notifications-watch-app-other-features/57853). None of them identified as medical.
  - `[SAID]` The one medical mention goes the other way: a commenter says medical students always have reviews due, so they don't need a daily reminder ([2025](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/functional-notifications-are-essential-for-ankimobile/66148)).
  - `[SAID]` Anki's developer doubts passive exposure helps without active recall ([2025](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ios-widgets-notifications-watch-app-other-features/57853)).
- **Single facts outside the big deck happen, though nobody describes wanting a separate app for them.**
  - `[CLAIMED]` Make a quick card for each missed question (Blueprint, above).
  - `[SAID]` One student made a card for every wrong UWorld question ([SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/is-anking-too-much-with-p-f-step-1.1444655/)) `[OLD]`.
  - `[SAID]` AnKing, plus a school deck, plus own cards for weak spots ([AnkiHub, 2025](https://community.ankihub.net/t/looking-for-best-anki-decks-for-medical-students/408019)).
  - `[CLAIMED]` For residents: 10-20 minutes a day, 50-100 curated cards, built from patients seen and missed questions ([Residency Advisor, Feb 2025](https://residencyadvisor.com/resources/residency-survival-tips/maximize-learning-best-practices-for-efficient-medical-study-habits), promotes its own tool).
  - `[SAID]` One blogger made cards during the day on the wards ([2019](https://mullenmemory.com/memory-palace/tag/srs), excerpt only) `[OLD]`.
  - I found no thread about pearls from patients kept for years after boards.

### What volume implies for a small app that doesn't handle volume

All of this is `[INFERRED]` from the numbers above.

- **One card at a time can't carry a Step deck.** 400 reviews a day spread over 16 waking hours is one every 2.4 minutes; 700 is one every 1.4 minutes. At the 18 seconds a card one poster's numbers imply, 400 reviews is still 2 hours of sitting.
- **The Step deck isn't only cards.** It comes with shared updates, tags and suspension workflows, and that is what people pay AnkiHub for. It is also 35,000 cards. Moving it elsewhere would lose what they pay for.
- **Time is the scarce resource.** A busy medic's stated problem is too many reviews. Any tool that adds reviews would meet that objection first, and in this group the advice is to cut, not add.
- **The lower-volume groups are the ones where the numbers fit:** clinical years (120-200 a day, still heavy), residents (5-20 new cards, 10-20 minutes, decks of hundreds to a few thousand), and own cards from missed questions or patients. Evidence for all of them is thin, and much of it comes from sellers.
- **Exam deadlines dominate.** Pre-clinical students and residents alike study toward Step, shelf and in-training exams, so non-exam cards compete with what is graded.

## Evidence against

- **Against "Anki causes burnout":** Wothe found no link to stress or burnout, and better sleep among users (extract). Forum posts over-represent people in trouble. One reports 90 minutes a day and fine results (SDN extract) `[OLD]`, and FSRS is praised for cutting load (Reddit copy).
- **Against "Anki drives scores":** no randomized trial exists. `[SAID]` Halperin found no correlation between third-party resources and Step 1; the Ireland cohort and IAMSE found no overall advantage.
- **Against "small moments are how medics review":** the documented routine is long daily blocks, plus remotes. Only 2 old SDN posters and 1 study mention gaps, and phones on rounds carry a stigma.
- **Against "they want reminders":** the only medical mention says they don't need them, and I found no medic asking for a widget.
- **Against "volume is unavoidable":** residents and clinical-year students report much lower loads, and pass/fail Step 1 pushed some to partial decks. One [seller](https://residencyadvisor.com/resources/med-school-life/is-anki-enough-for-usmle-what-the-evidence-and-outcomes-show) recommends 8,000-15,000 cards over 30,000+.
- **Against "everyone in medicine uses it":** 25.2% in Jordan. US-heavy forums skew this whole picture, and I saw little from Latin America or Europe beyond Ireland.
