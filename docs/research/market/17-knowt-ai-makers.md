# 17 knowt-ai-makers
Sources read: 51 (app pages, Trustpilot pages, press, studies, forum threads; most through WebFetch's summary). Covered from 2023 to 2026.

Ratings and reviews below were read 2026-11-05. "TP" is Trustpilot. Store dates give no year; I assume 2026. Counts of complaints are of the reviews shown on the page, not all of them. Unless marked otherwise, in the sheets features, prices and user numbers are [CLAIMED], and reviews and study results [SAID].

## Findings

### Sheets

**Knowt.** For US high-school and college students, AP above all. Free flashcards with unlimited Learn mode, Quizlet import, AI summarizers, lecture recording, voice tutor. Site's first line: "Every AI Study Tool You Need for a 4.0"; store: "Discover a new way to study with Knowt, the all-in-one study app…" [CLAIMED] ([site](https://knowt.com/), [store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/knowt-ai-flashcards-notes/id6463744184)). Claims 8.9M users on the site, 7M in the store. Price: $7.99/month billed $95.99 a year, or $24.99 monthly ([plans](https://knowt.com/plans)); the store lists $9.99 weekly and annual $59.99 to $149.99. A directory read $149.99 a year in October 2026, so it varies ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/knowt/pricing), affiliate-style directory, snippet). Store 4.7 (11K). TP 2.6, but only 8 reviews: AI quality about 3 of 6 shown, billing 1 ([TP](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/knowt.com)). Reviewers cite flipped definitions and lost cards; few paywall complaints ([Unstar, June 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/quizlet-anki-brainscape-knowt-studysmarter-flashcard-study-apps-ranked-2026), sells review analytics). Widgets since 4.0.0: Quick Create and Record, not review. Streak pop-ups drew a complaint, patched. No watch mentioned. Alive: 4.3.0, Oct 26.

**Why people move and leave, across these apps.** Thin evidence, no good counts. They come to skip making cards (SmartFlash's 6 students; Brown's 74%, below) and, for Knowt, for free modes and Quizlet import [INFERRED]. ### What they charge and how they limit

- Store prices vary widely inside each app, which suggests price tests: Gizmo 8 prices, Turbo 9, Knowt 7 [INFERRED]; Jungle's 7 are plain tiers. Turbo's founder confirms A/B tests of prices [SAID, 1, [TechCrunch, Oct 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users/)].
- 4 of 7 specialist apps sell a weekly plan ($6.99 to $13.99): Knowt, Gizmo, Turbo, Jungle. That fits exam cramming [INFERRED].
- Limit types, 5 kinds: uploads or generations a month (Jungle, StudyFetch), processing hours (Turbo), an undisclosed monthly allowance (Knowt: its [help page](https://help.knowt.com/en/articles/10714626-how-do-i-see-how-much-ai-i-m-using) shows only a countdown), friction (Gizmo's hearts and waits), token-based credits (RemNote). Mindgrasp has only a trial.
- Reviewers' main grievance is the charge, not the limit: billing was the top or tied-top theme on TP for Mindgrasp, Turbo, Revisely, StudyFetch and Gizmo (5 of 6 profiles); only Knowt's top theme was AI quality, on few reviews [SAID]. Trial-to-annual charges recur ([TP Mindgrasp](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/mindgrasp.ai)).

### Do people trust AI cards, and how good are they?

- No survey I found measures trust in AI cards specifically. Closest: 253 students, 47% trust generative AI, 16% distrust ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04631v2), snippet, [OLD 2023]). Six students in a design study wanted editable, transparent output, seeing AI as a partner to check ([SmartFlash, Feb 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14431), tiny sample).
- Errors, measured by experts on medicine and biology:
  - Radiology: about 1.0% of 1,284 reviewed cards were wrong even after AI checking, above the team's 0.3% limit ([Asan Medical Center release, Aug 2026](https://news-en.amc.seoul.kr/news/eng/detail.do?cntId=3283), npj Digital Medicine).
  - ChatGPT practice questions (not cards): 22% factual errors, 49% item-writing flaws, of 65 ([Advances in Physiology Education, June 2025](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/LLM-Generated-Multiple-Choice-Practice-Quizzes-for/991022059818004721)).
  - A biologist found 2 errors in 100 Claude cards, plus a later reversed gradient that Gemini failed to catch as checker; he stopped generating cards for topics he can't verify ([blog, June 2025](https://denovo.substack.com/p/ai-generated-anki-cards-are-not-to), sells nothing).
- Unverified numbers to ignore: "36% unusable" (snippet, no source); Knowt "90%" against Gizmo "85%" accuracy ([TechRaisal, Oct 2026](https://www.techraisal.com/blog/gizmo-ai-vs-knowt-i-used-both-for-a-full-semester-and-who-actually-wins-in-2026), no method, a directory selling listings).
- Against making none yourself: in 5 of 6 experiments students learned more from self-made than premade cards, about 10 points, up to 25%; paraphrasing was best, copy-paste showed no significant edge ([Pan et al., via Tech & Learning, Feb 2024](https://www.techlearning.com/news/tech-tools-provide-flashcards-to-students-thats-not-a-good-thing-says-new-research) [OLD]; [design details](https://michellemillerphd.substack.com/p/r3-21-january-8-2024-do-students) [OLD]). It tested premade cards, not AI: extending it is [INFERRED].
- Over 20,000 creators: those typing by hand studied 39% longer, spent over twice as long per card, reviewed nearly 3 times as often ([Brainscape, about Jan 2026](https://m.brainscape.com/academy/flashcard-copilot-ai-generator), sells an AI copilot; says itself this isn't controlled) [CLAIMED]. Self-selection could explain it [INFERRED].
- Anki forum: 1 post found prompting nearly as slow as writing by hand, with mediocre results, 0 replies ([Dec 2025](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/anki-ai-card-generators/67940)). Older posts argue writing cards is itself learning (search-level, 2 threads). A Reddit user says AI inflated deck sizes (1, second-hand, seen through a search snippet).

### Is anyone paying for just this?

- Yes for bundles, little evidence for generation alone. Turbo: about $500K a month by March 2025, $13M lifetime, 10 people, profitable, self-reported, relayed from Business Insider ([summary, June 2026](https://letsdatascience.com/news/founders-scale-ai-study-app-to-13m-revenue-41e281e9)). But it sells notes first. Gizmo: #104 top-grossing iPhone, March 2026 ([PricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1610516671)), with no revenue known.
- Apps closest to "cards only" look small: Jungle about 1K ratings, Revisely 54 TP reviews, against Turbo's 34K and Gizmo's 17K [INFERRED; ratings aren't revenue]. A free generator's maker reported 180 users, 8 engaged, 0% day-7 retention, $0 revenue in 28 days ([Show HN, about March 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47203567), 1 case).
- Some of the payment looks involuntary: unexpected-charge reviews dominate 2 of the profiles above [INFERRED].

### What a generation costs them

- No app publishes this; I found none stated, and TechCrunch's piece on Turbo doesn't mention it. All below is [INFERRED].
- Best proxy: RemNote sells 1,000 runs of 250 words for $10, so about 1 cent a run at retail, its cost being lower.
- From list prices ([Finout, Oct 2026](https://www.finout.io/blog/llm-token-cost-by-model-2026-pricing-data-and-optimization-tips?hs_amp=true), sells spend tools): a job of 2,000 tokens in and 1,000 out (my assumption, some pages into a set of cards) costs about $0.0007 on GPT-6 Luna, $0.014 on Claude Sonnet 5. A 300-page book, 200,000 in and 30,000 out: about $0.04 to $0.70. Lecture audio and chat aren't counted.
- So a card run is cheap against $8 to $20 a month; caps look more like a paywall lever and a guard against heavy users than a response to one run's cost. Turbo's and Knowt's costlier features, recording and voice, may weigh more.

## Evidence against

- Against "users distrust these apps": store ratings run 4.6 to 4.8 across thousands of ratings, and StudyFetch holds 4.1 on TP. Trustpilot draws the angry, and says several of these profiles may not be representative. Gizmo's and Knowt's TP samples are under 10. But app ratings are also prompted in-app [INFERRED].
- Against "AI cards hurt learning": at Brown, AI Anki cards from a tuned prompt showed no significant exam difference (p = 0.86 genetics, p = 0.05 pharmacology), and 74% reported saving time ([medRxiv preprint, 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.13.25327518v1.full.pdf), snippet only). Heavier users rated them closer to student-made cards. A 2023 study reportedly found AI and human cards comparable in a MOOC (cited second-hand, [OLD]). No randomized test of AI against self-made cards turned up either way.
- Against "errors are the big risk": the 1% radiology rate is low in itself, and that study's team generated 6,000 cards; 91% of the flawed-quiz study's questions were a reasonable start for revising. Errors matter where the learner can't check them; the biologist still finds cards useful for what he half knows.
- Against "AI is free now, so these die": no evidence of mass switching from these apps to ChatGPT or Gemini; Gizmo raised and Turbo grew after both launched. Against the opposite too: no evidence of who pays because of a scheduler.
- Against "accuracy is what users complain about": Unstar's reading of 5 flashcard apps puts accuracy at only 12% of 1-3 star complaints, with no total given.
- The 53% of medical students "who'd use ChatGPT for Anki cards", cited on the forum, I couldn't confirm: the PLOS paper's snippet shows 52% had used ChatGPT for coursework, a different question ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39092290/), [OLD 2023 survey]).
