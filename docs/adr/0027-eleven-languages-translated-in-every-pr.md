# Ficha comes in eleven languages besides English, translated in every PR

ADR 0009 made English the app's base language and left other languages for later. On 2026-11-01, grilling #390, Lucas decided Ficha comes in the most-used languages before his friends test it (#359), since not all of them speak English. The languages are chosen by how much each matters to a paid app on the Mac, from what its countries spend on the App Store, how many Macs they have and its weight on the web, not by how many people speak it: Simplified Chinese, Japanese, German, Spanish, French, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Traditional Chinese, Italian, Russian and Arabic. Claude translates them, with Anki's word for each term Anki has, in the same PR that adds or changes a text, so no screen mixes languages. This changes ADR 0009, which left the languages for later and planned Rioplatense Spanish first.

## Considered options

- The most-spoken languages, as Lucas first listed them, with Hindi and Urdu: few people use a Mac in them, Anki has only about 20% of its Hindi and 6% of its Urdu translated, nobody can check them, and macOS doesn't offer Urdu as a system language. They can join the Mac and the iPhone together if the App Store shows people from India or Pakistan.
- A translation service: it costs money and slows every change.
- Translations in batches before each release, as in Anki, whose volunteers translate its new texts later: each PR would cost a little less, but the app would show English among the other languages in between.
- A neutral Spanish with "tú" for every country: it reads natural everywhere, but Lucas prefers Argentine Spanish.
- A Rioplatense Spanish for Argentina beside a neutral one: each PR would write Spanish twice, and a Mac set to Spanish (Latin America) can get the general one anyway, even with its region set to Argentina.

## Consequences

- One Spanish, `es`, for every Mac set to Spanish, in Rioplatense Spanish with "vos", though it reads unusual in Mexico or Spain. Both Chinese, since a Mac in Traditional Chinese doesn't fall back to Simplified.
- The words: Anki's translation wherever Anki has the term, such as deck, card, the four ratings and interval; Apple's for the Mac's own words, such as Settings and Quit; and for Ficha's own terms, such as capture and inbox, one word in each language, kept in a list in the repo so every PR uses the same.
- `CODING_STANDARDS.md`'s rule that every text can be translated becomes one that every text is: each PR that adds or changes a text the app shows translates it into the eleven languages, and `make check` fails when a language lacks a text. Apple ships no command that checks a String Catalog for missing translations, so the check is our own.
- Friends who speak a language correct it during the beta through TestFlight's feedback, and Lucas reviews the Spanish. A language none of them speaks ships as Claude translated it.
- In Arabic the whole interface is mirrored, as in the apps that support Arabic and as Apple's frameworks do by default, and the keys keep their ratings: 1 is still again. A card's content follows the direction of its own text whatever the app's language. Apple documents nothing about mirroring a menu-bar item or a panel, so the review panel and the menu are checked in the pictures.
- `make render` draws a window in any of the languages. The PR that brings the translations shows every window in German, the longest, and in Arabic; each later PR that changes what Lucas sees shows it in both beside English. A text that doesn't fit makes its view grow instead of being cut.
- The translations cover the Mac app and its widget, both with the engine's messages, and the iPhone app, which does the same (ADR 0019). The `ficha` CLI and Ficha Dev's debug tools stay in English (ADR 0009). The App Store page and the privacy policy in `site/` are translated when the store page is made, and TestFlight's description for the friends is written in #359, in their languages.
- Dates, numbers and durations follow the device's region, through Apple's formats.
- The names Ficha comes with, the Default deck and the four card types, show in each device's language. A deck at the top level can't be named Default, and a card type can't take a built-in type's name, in English or in the device's language. So a deck named "Standard" on a Spanish iPhone shows on a German Mac beside Default, which German calls "Standard" too, and a card type does the same. Lucas accepted it as rare.
- Standard practice: String Catalogs, Apple's mirroring for right-to-left languages, and the device's region for dates, numbers and durations. Our own: languages chosen by their market; one Rioplatense Spanish for every country; every text translated in its own PR, with our own check; Anki's words in each language; languages nobody on the team speaks shipped unreviewed; German and Arabic pictures in every PR that changes what Lucas sees.
