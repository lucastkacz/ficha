# Subjects are tags, not decks

Lucas sometimes wants subjects apart: the topics an exam covers first (#36), or a course he's done with out of his reviews (#154). Anki splits cards into decks, each card in one, each deck with its own settings and studied on its own. Ficha keeps a single stream of cards and gives tags two switches instead: a priority tag brings its cards first, and a tag set aside takes its cards out of reviews until Lucas brings it back. Appearances are short and spread through the day, so mixing subjects is the point, and Anki's own manual keeps decks for broad categories, since related cards reviewed together are easy to guess from context. Decks would also double the settings and screens on the Mac and the iPhone. Burying every card of a finished subject, with nothing new, was the other option: it mixes "not for now" with "not anymore", and misses the cards that get the tag later.

## Consequences

- A card can have several tags, so a tag set aside wins over the others: the card leaves reviews when any of its tags is set aside, a priority tag among them or not.
- A tag is plain, a priority tag or set aside, never two of them.
- A flagged card waits in the inbox and a buried one stays buried, whatever their tags.
- Which tags are priority tags or set aside is stored with the cards, not as a setting of each device, so the backups keep it and iCloud sync (#161) will carry it.
- Nothing reviews one tag alone: a priority tag brings its cards first, and the others still come when there's room.
- A set-aside card's wait keeps running, as a buried card's does, so bringing a tag back after a while brings a backlog, which drains as ADR 0003 says.
